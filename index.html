01 / RESEARCH

The physics of
light and charge.

We connect atomic-scale structure to how light-generated charges localise, move and recombine in emerging solar absorbers.

01 / LOCALISATION

Charge-carrier localisation in emerging solar absorbers

We identify the structural conditions that trigger ultrafast carrier localisation, and how cation chemistry can sustain photoconductivity in perovskite-inspired absorbers.

Key paper: Overcoming Charge-Carrier Localization in Metal Chalcohalides (JACS, 2026)
↗
02 / BAND STRUCTURE

Pseudo-direct semiconductors

In Bi2S3, a nearby direct transition supports strong absorption while a lower-energy indirect state delays electron-hole recombination, pointing to a new design route for solar absorbers.

Key paper: Delayed electron-hole recombination in a pseudo-direct semiconductor (Nature, 2026)
↗
03 / DISORDER

Cation disorder in Ag-Bi perovskite-inspired materials

We showed that a more homogeneous Ag/Bi cation distribution in AgBiS2 nanocrystal films suppresses carrier localisation and raises mobility sixfold.

Key paper: Cation-Disorder Engineering Promotes Efficient Charge-Carrier Transport in AgBiS2 Nanocrystal Films (Advanced Materials, 2023)

02 / APPROACH

Following charge
in real time.

Optical pump-terahertz probe (OPTP) spectroscopy follows charge transport after a semiconductor absorbs light, without adding electrical contacts.

  1. 01

    Optical pump. A femtosecond light pulse creates electrons and holes in the thin film.

  2. 02

    THz probe. A broadband terahertz pulse crosses the same spot after a controlled delay. Mobile charges change its transmitted electric field.

  3. 03

    Readout. Comparing pumped and unpumped THz waveforms reveals transient photoconductivity. How it varies with delay and frequency helps distinguish transport, localisation and recombination.
