We study how photoexcited charge carriers move, localise and recombine in lead-halide and lead-free perovskites, metal chalcohalides and other perovskite-inspired semiconductors, using ultrafast optical and terahertz spectroscopy.

01 / RESEARCH

Research

The physics of light and charge.

We relate composition, cation ordering and electron–phonon coupling to the mobility, localisation and recombination of photogenerated carriers in emerging solar absorbers.

01 / LOCALISATION

Charge-carrier localisation in Bi- and Sb-based absorbers

In many perovskite-inspired semiconductors, photoexcited carriers self-localise within picoseconds, which limits their mobility. We study which structural and chemical factors drive this, such as cation disorder, octahedral connectivity and lattice softness, and which ones prevent it, by measuring thin-film series with systematic changes in composition using temperature-dependent terahertz and transient absorption spectroscopy.

02 / BAND STRUCTURE

Pseudo-direct semiconductors

In Bi2S3, a direct transition close in energy to the band edge gives strong absorption, while a slightly lower indirect state delays electron–hole recombination. Within the TRAILS project we are looking for other semiconductors with this band structure and testing how far it can extend carrier lifetimes without sacrificing absorption.

Background Delayed electron-hole recombination in a pseudo-direct semiconductor (Nature, 2026)

03 / TRANSPORT

Transport across layers in 2D perovskites

In layered metal-halide perovskites, excitations move far more slowly between the inorganic layers than within them, yet out-of-plane transport is what a vertical device needs. We measure out-of-plane diffusion in thin films with time-resolved photoluminescence and relate it to the organic spacer, including odd–even effects of its chain length.

Background Odd–Even Cation Engineering of the Excitation Transport Anisotropy in Two-Dimensional Perovskite Films (ACS Nano, 2026)

02 / MATERIALS

Perovskite-inspired materials

A clearer view of what new materials can do.

Lead-halide perovskites absorb light strongly and transport charge efficiently even when they are processed from solution at low temperature and contain many defects. Their lead content and limited stability have driven a search for materials that keep these properties while using less toxic, more stable elements.

Perovskite-inspired materials (PIMs) have been found in three ways: by chemical analogy, replacing Pb2+ with other cations; by structural analogy, keeping the perovskite framework; and by electronic analogy. The electronic route looks for cations with an ns2 lone pair, such as Bi3+, Sb3+ and Sn2+, whose filled s orbitals shape the band edges as those of Pb2+ do and are thought to make the material tolerant to defects. Many of the resulting compounds do not have a perovskite structure at all.

These materials also bring their own loss mechanisms. Absorption near the band edge is often weak, many have indirect band gaps, and strong coupling between charges and the lattice can localise photoexcited carriers within picoseconds. These processes unfold on femtosecond-to-nanosecond timescales, which is why we study them with ultrafast spectroscopy.

Main families

  • Halide double perovskitese.g. Cs2AgBiBr6
  • Ag–Bi–I rudorffitese.g. AgBiI4, Ag2BiI5
  • ABZ2 chalcogenidese.g. AgBiS2 nanocrystals
  • Metal chalcohalidese.g. BiSI, SbSI
  • Pnictogen chalcogenidese.g. Bi2S3, Sb2Se3
  • Low-dimensional Bi and Sb halidese.g. Cs3Bi2I9

03 / METHODS

Methods

Following charge in real time.

Optical pump-terahertz probe (OPTP) spectroscopy follows charge transport after a semiconductor absorbs light, without adding electrical contacts. A dedicated OPTP spectrometer is under construction in our Padova lab.

  1. 01

    Optical pump. A femtosecond light pulse creates electrons and holes in the thin film.

  2. 02

    THz probe. A broadband terahertz pulse crosses the same spot after a controlled delay. Mobile charges change its transmitted electric field.

  3. 03

    Readout. Comparing pumped and unpumped THz waveforms gives the transient photoconductivity and the effective charge-carrier mobility. Its decay with delay tracks recombination, and its frequency dependence distinguishes free from localised carriers.

Facilities

Instruments built and run in Padova.

Femtosecond transient absorption

Pump–probe spectroscopy with shot-to-shot white-light detection (sCMOS camera on an imaging spectrograph, 1 kHz).

Operational · upgrading

Time-resolved photoluminescence

Operational

Two-dimensional electronic spectroscopy
